REAL-WORLD CONTEXT YOU MUST USE
==================================================

Use these as strategic and product constraints:

1. MARKET REALITY
- Government paperwork is not just a form-filling problem; it is a trust, interpretation, eligibility, documentation, and rejection-prevention problem.
- QueueCutter should not compete as a generic “AI assistant.”
- It must compete as a “submission readiness and case preparation layer” for difficult public workflows.

2. COMPETITOR REALITY
- Propel / Providers is a serious adjacent benchmark in SNAP. It serves millions of SNAP households and has user value beyond forms through EBT balance checking, suspicious activity alerts, and fraud-related utility.
- DoNotPay is a cautionary benchmark: broad “Swiss Army Knife” positioning plus “Robot Lawyer” branding created trust, compliance, and credibility backlash.
- Diia is a benchmark for unified citizen-facing government workflow infrastructure and shows the value of consolidating fragmented public-service tasks into a single trusted system.

3. COMPLIANCE REALITY
- Unauthorized Practice of Law (UPL) risk is real. QueueCutter must be framed as workflow facilitation, drafting assistance, evidence organization, and official-form preparation — NOT legal advice or legal representation.
- AI-generated inferences may be regulated as personal information.
- Security, explainability, accessibility, and auditability are not optional extras.

4. PRODUCT REALITY
- Demo features are not enough.
- Trust, source verification, explanation traceability, edge-case handling, and failure recovery are required for real adoption.
- If the app cannot cope with unstable government systems, low-literacy users, or contradictory documents, it fails in production.
